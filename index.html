The Mandate

AI capability is becoming a requirement for competitiveness.

The organisations that benefit most from AI will not simply be the ones that buy the most tools. They will be the ones whose leaders and teams understand how to use them, where to use them and where not to use them.

My role is to help leaders and organisations understand AI, use it properly, protect sensitive information and improve real work. The purpose is not to remove people from the organisation. It is to equip people to perform better with AI.

The Challenge

Five places AI quietly helps — or hurts.

Risk

Uncontrolled AI use can expose confidential data, create security problems and introduce unreliable information into important decisions.

Profit

AI investment without a clear business purpose simply becomes another expense.

Efficiency

Teams keep spending hours on work that could be done faster with the right AI systems.

Delivery

Slow processes affect customers, management and the organisation's ability to execute.

Governance

Without clear rules, employees use AI differently and leadership loses visibility over how company information is handled.

Across Africa, 82% of organisations are running AI pilots, yet only 41% have a clearly defined AI roadmap and 37% have formalised responsible-AI processes. — Source: PwC Africa, 2026.

The Approach

Tools are easy to buy. Capability is harder to build.

Buying AI software does not create AI capability. Leaders need to understand what the technology can do. Employees need to know how to use it without exposing sensitive information.

Departments need practical use cases tied to the work they already perform, and leadership needs governance and visibility. Organisations that build this capability will move faster and compete harder than those that only buy tools.
